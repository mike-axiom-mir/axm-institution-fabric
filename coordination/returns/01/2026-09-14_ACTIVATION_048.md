## Research conclusion

Decision 021 left three concrete continuity choices:

1. immutable/snapshot-like observation context;
2. retained verified bytes for positive exact observations;
3. explicit context-bound/non-reexecution standing for live observations.

The first Decision 023 slice selects option 3.

The reason is not that snapshots or retained bytes are undesirable. They are stronger future evidence mechanisms, but neither is required to state the next bounded fact truthfully. The current `FilesystemObjectStore` root is mutable runtime state; therefore the universal kernel must not record its path and silently call it immutable replay context. Instead, the observation fact explicitly says it was made against a `live_mutable_store` and that literal observation `reexecution_standing = not_established`.

This preserves a critical distinction:

- **institutional replay** may later consume the immutable recorded observation fact as historical input;
- **literal re-execution** of the original live filesystem observation is not claimed unless a future snapshot/context contract makes that possible.

## Why snapshots are deferred

A real immutable/snapshot-like context would need its own grounded identity and completeness semantics. In particular, replaying historical negative observations such as absence requires more than naming a filesystem directory: it requires a durable statement of what state was frozen and how that freeze is identified. That is additional kernel machinery, not a free property of content-addressed filenames.

Opening that machinery now would enlarge Decision 023 beyond the smallest v0 proof.

## Why retained bytes are deferred

A successful exact `load_bytes()` already establishes, for that invocation, that bytes were obtained and reproduced the exact immutable ref. Decision 023 can record that fact without copying source payload bytes into the observation record.

A later retained-byte evidence contract may strengthen independent future verification. Decision 023 explicitly does not claim those bytes are retained or that future availability is guaranteed.
